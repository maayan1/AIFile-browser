# Editorial Review: File Browser 2.0 PRD (v2) and Addendum

**Purpose read:** these two documents exist to help the author, non-technical stakeholders, and the architecture and story-planning phases agree on exactly what the File Browser 2.0 MVP must do (PRD) and on the technical guidance that comes with it (addendum).

**Structure models:** PRD judged against *Strategic/Context (Pyramid)*. Addendum judged against *Explanation (Conceptual)*, the closest fit for a set of guidance notes.

**Verdict: Pass with revisions.** Both documents are well built for their purpose. The PRD opens with a one-line promise, a reading guide and the vision. Requirements follow one schema with phase, dependencies and testable criteria. Assumptions are tagged inline and indexed. The issues are about expression, not shape: the word "version" means three different things, a few glossary terms are not used exactly as defined, some acronyms and AI terms are never explained to non-technical readers, and §7 and §8.2 overlap. No scope changes are proposed. Content is treated as sacrosanct.

**Stylistic choices to preserve (prose pass):** short declarative sentences; plain, slightly informal tone with contractions ("can't", "that's"); named user personas with concrete quoted queries; bold for key numbers; inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags; tables for dense comparisons. Technical vocabulary in the addendum (sidecar, stdin/stdout, loopback, float32) suits its architecture audience and is not flagged.

**Severity** (added at the caller's request; the review lenses do not rank findings by default):
- **High:** a reader is likely to misunderstand scope, status or a requirement.
- **Medium:** slows comprehension or makes story writing less precise.
- **Low:** polish or consistency.

Overlap between passes is noted in the Changes column. It is signal, not duplication.
